BAMC 2027 Public Lecture

The BAMC 2027 Public Lecture will be delivered by Professor Des Higham FRSE FIMA (University of Edinburgh).

About the Speaker

Professor Des Higham OBE is one of the United Kingdom’s leading applied mathematicians and an internationally recognised advocate for mathematics. He is Professor of Numerical Analysis at the University of Edinburgh, where his research spans numerical analysis, scientific computing, artificial intelligence, data science and network science. His work has made fundamental contributions to stochastic computation and the development of mathematical methods for understanding complex systems and emerging technologies.

Alongside his research achievements, Professor Higham is widely known for his outstanding commitment to public engagement. Through books, articles, blogs, public lectures and outreach activities, he has helped make modern mathematics accessible to diverse audiences and has showcased its central role in science, technology and everyday life.

A Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and recipient of major international awards for his contributions to numerical analysis, Professor Higham is particularly valued for his ability to combine mathematical depth with clear, engaging communication. His public lectures are renowned for revealing the power, beauty and relevance of mathematics to a wide audience.

Lecture Title

When AI Goes Awry

Abstract

We are all aware that artificial intelligence (AI) can be remarkably impressive in certain well-defined scenarios. However, over the last decade, researchers have repeatedly shown that AI can also be easily fooled. These vulnerabilities raise issues regarding safety, reliability and interpretability; especially in high risk settings. From a practical perspective, there has been a war of escalation between those developing attack and defence strategies. I will present examples of algorithms that have been used to fool convolutional neural networks, transformer models and large language models and I will explain why mathematics and statistics are at the heart of this area.
