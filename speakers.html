Plenary Speakers

BAMC 2027 is delighted to welcome five internationally recognised researchers whose work spans the breadth of contemporary applied mathematics.

Professor David Abrahams CBE

Isaac Newton Institute for Mathematical Sciences, University of Cambridge

Professor David Abrahams CBE is one of the United Kingdom’s most distinguished applied mathematicians and has played a leading role in shaping the modern mathematical sciences landscape. His research has advanced the theoretical understanding of wave propagation and complex physical systems, with influential contributions spanning acoustics, elasticity, fracture mechanics, electromagnetism, water waves and scientific modelling.

Alongside his outstanding research achievements, Professor Abrahams has provided exceptional leadership to the mathematical sciences community. He served as Scientific Director of the International Centre for Mathematical Sciences (ICMS) in Edinburgh and later as Director of the Isaac Newton Institute for Mathematical Sciences in Cambridge, two of the world's leading centres for mathematical research and collaboration. He has also served as President of the Institute of Mathematics and its Applications (IMA).

His contributions to mathematics, industrial engagement and the wider research community have been recognised through numerous honours, including the IMA Gold Medal for outstanding contributions to mathematics and its applications. Widely respected for his vision, leadership and scientific excellence, Professor Abrahams has become one of the most influential figures in contemporary applied mathematics.

Professor Tiina Roose

University of Southampton

Professor Tiina Roose is an internationally recognised leader in biological and environmental modelling whose research lies at the interface of mathematics, biology and environmental science. She combines mathematical modelling, computation and experiment to understand complex multiscale processes in living systems and the natural environment.

Her work has transformed our understanding of how plants and soils interact, providing fundamental insight into nutrient uptake, root–soil interactions and the functioning of biological transport networks. Through extensive interdisciplinary collaboration, she has demonstrated how mathematical modelling can contribute to major global challenges in sustainable agriculture, food security and environmental resilience.

Professor Laurette Tuckerman

CNRS and ESPCI Paris

Professor Laurette Tuckerman is internationally recognised for her pioneering contributions to fluid dynamics, nonlinear dynamics and scientific computing. Her research has provided fundamental insight into hydrodynamic instability, transition to turbulence and pattern formation, helping to shape modern understanding of complex fluid flows.

Through the development of innovative computational and bifurcation methods, she has influenced a broad range of areas across applied mathematics and physics. Her work on fluid instabilities, convection and turbulent flows has been particularly influential, establishing her as one of the leading figures in theoretical and computational fluid dynamics.

Professor Chris Breward

University of Oxford

Professor Chris Breward is a leading applied mathematician whose work lies at the interface of mathematics, industry and the life sciences. His research uses mathematical modelling to address challenges arising in manufacturing, fluid mechanics, medicine and technological processes, with a particular focus on problems motivated by real-world applications.

He has an outstanding record of collaboration with industry and has played a major role in strengthening links between academia and external partners. As co-Director of Oxford's Centre for Doctoral Training in Industrially Focused Mathematical Modelling and Scientific Director of the UK Knowledge Exchange Hub for Mathematical Sciences, he has been a leading advocate for the application of mathematics to industrial and societal challenges.

Professor Anna Pandolfi

Politecnico di Milano

Professor Anna Pandolfi is an internationally recognised leader in computational mechanics and biomechanics. Her research has advanced the development of mathematical and computational methods for modelling fracture, damage, contact phenomena and complex materials, with applications spanning engineering, materials science and biomedical systems.

She has made particularly influential contributions to the computational modelling of biological tissues and the mechanics of failure in solids. Through pioneering work at the interface of mechanics, mathematics and computation, she has helped shape modern approaches to understanding how materials and living tissues deform, interact and fail under complex conditions.
